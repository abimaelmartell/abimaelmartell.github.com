A few weeks ago I had the chance to attend **Platanus Hack ft. Firecrawl** in Mexico City, and it was an incredible experience. The hackathon brought together a hundred hackers from all over, and the energy in the room was electric. I got to represent [Firecrawl](https://firecrawl.dev) and see firsthand how teams used our tool to build some amazing projects.

![The opening ceremony of Platanus Hack 26](/assets/images/platanus-hack-2026/venue-opening.jpg){:.credit-event}

*Kicking things off — 100 hackers, 36 hours, de cero a producto*

[Firecrawl](https://firecrawl.dev) sponsored the event, which was organized entirely by [Platanus](http://platan.us/) and hosted by [Fintual](https://www.fintual.com/). I finally got to meet the Platanus team in person, and they were incredibly welcoming and supportive. They made sure everything ran smoothly, and their passion for building a real developer community was obvious the whole weekend.

## A hundred hackers in one room

The hackathon was capped at 100 participants, selected from hundreds of applicants across Mexico. What impressed me most was the diversity: people from China, the US, Bolivia, Colombia, Chile, and of course, México — with ages starting at 14 and going all the way up.

![A hundred hackers in one room, hands up](/assets/images/platanus-hack-2026/hackers-room.jpg){:.credit-event}

*This is what the whole weekend felt like*

## Meeting the people behind the projects

The event was organized around tracks, which were revealed at the opening. Teams had to pick one on the spot — legacy, new media, new interfaces, or AI security — and shape their entire product idea around it. I love this format because nobody arrives with a pre-built project; everything you see at the end was genuinely built over the weekend.

![Heads-down and shipping](/assets/images/platanus-hack-2026/hackers-working.jpg){:.credit-event}

*Heads-down and shipping*

Throughout the event I walked the rooms and talked with teams about their ideas and how Firecrawl could help them. I was amazed by the creativity and ingenuity in that building. Representing Firecrawl meant I spent most of the weekend answering questions, debugging alongside teams, and just nerding out about web data with people who were genuinely excited to use it.

![A hacker smiling in a brand-new Firecrawl cap](/assets/images/platanus-hack-2026/firecrawl-cap.jpg){:.credit-event}

*The caps went fast*

## Seeing Firecrawl in the wild

Which brings me to the thing I cared most about: seeing how teams actually used Firecrawl. It's one thing to build the product; it's another to watch people reach for it under time pressure and bend it toward problems we never anticipated.

![Me on stage opening the Firecrawl talk](/assets/images/platanus-hack-2026/pitch-title.jpg){:.credit-own} ![Walking through what you can build with web data](/assets/images/platanus-hack-2026/pitch-build.jpg)

*Giving the Firecrawl talk — dale acceso web a tus agentes*

I saw some genuinely great usage over the weekend — teams pulling in web data to power their ideas, wiring scraping and extraction into their projects, and shipping working demos in a matter of hours. Watching people get value out of something you've poured work into is, honestly, the best feedback loop there is.

Something that surprised me: even though this was our first event in LATAM, a good chunk of the 100 hackers already knew Firecrawl. A handful had even attended our previous events in SF, like Hack the Stackathon and our Founders Inc stand earlier this year. It's a great feeling to see the work travel that far.

### The Firecrawl Track winner: Sendero

We announced a small prize for the best use of Firecrawl, and the winner was **Sendero** — an AI-powered platform for missing persons research. They used Firecrawl to scrape government databases (messy HTML pages, some with PDFs, some with images), organized all of that data so it was actually searchable by name, and then layered an AI agent on top. They also analyzed news articles and other sources to identify risk areas and help families find their loved ones.

It was a powerful example of what web data can do when it's pointed at a real problem. Out of everything I saw that weekend, this was the project that stuck with me.

![Team Sendero, winners of the Firecrawl Track](/assets/images/platanus-hack-2026/sendero-team.jpg){:.credit-event}

*Team Sendero, winners of the Firecrawl Track*

The runner-up built a live fútbol analytics platform. They used Firecrawl to pull data on both teams during a live match and generate real-time predictions of the outcome. Very different problem, same pattern: fresh web data feeding an AI agent, built in a weekend.

![Demo day at Platanus Hack](/assets/images/platanus-hack-2026/demo-day.jpg){:.credit-event}

*Demo day: 36 hours of work compressed into a few minutes on stage*

## Meanwhile, outside: México was winning

The hackathon wasn't the only thing happening in the city that weekend. México beat Korea in the World Cup, and the streets exploded. We grabbed the camera and walked straight into the celebration — flags on every corner, luchador masks, pink smoke, entire families out on the avenue at midnight. Fitting, considering one of our finalist teams spent the weekend building live fútbol analytics.
